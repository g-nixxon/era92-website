Meridian&rsquo;s old site had been built by four different teams over three years. Every page
worked. None of them agreed with each other. The team knew what was wrong; they didn&rsquo;t
have the time to step back and re-architect the whole experience while still shipping product.

## The approach

We didn&rsquo;t start with the homepage. We started with the qualification funnel: what does a
&ldquo;great&rdquo; demo request look like when it lands in Sales&rsquo; queue, and what would
a visitor need to read, click, and self-identify with before sending one? Everything else
flowed from there &mdash; including, eventually, a homepage that didn&rsquo;t need to do all
the heavy lifting alone.

The design system grew alongside the site. By the end of week four we had a small set of
components &mdash; hero variants, feature blocks, social-proof modules, a pricing comparison
&mdash; that could be remixed to spin up a new landing page in a day. The team has shipped
twelve variants since launch.

> The fastest marketing site is the one your team can ship the twelfth landing page on without
> calling an agency.

## What we delivered

The site shipped on Next.js, hosted on Vercel, with content in a small headless CMS so the
marketing team owns updates without a deploy:

- A redesigned information architecture organized around three buyer personas the team had
  already validated internally.
- 18 reusable components, fully responsive, with usage examples in Storybook.
- A new pricing page that pre-qualifies prospects into the right plan tier before they reach
  the demo form.
- Analytics events on every meaningful interaction so the team can iterate based on real
  behavior, not opinions.
- A handoff doc, a Loom walkthrough for the marketing team, and two weeks of pair-engineering
  support after launch.

## What we learned

You don&rsquo;t need to redesign every page to fix a site. The biggest single move on this
project was rewriting the homepage hero and the pricing page. Together those two pages account
for 80% of the conversion lift &mdash; everything else compounded but didn&rsquo;t move the
headline number. Knowing which pages carry the load is its own piece of work.
